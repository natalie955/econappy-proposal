Revised Approach

Measure → Continue

In response to your brief, the original flat eight-function build has been replaced by this structure. Only the first workflow is scoped and priced. Everything after it is quoted separately, once the one before it has proven its value.

Tested against real numbers you already know, then reviewed for two weeks against the KPI agreed up front.

Kill point

Continue?

We only go on to the next workflow if the last one earned it. Stopping here is a legitimate outcome, not a failure.

Technology should not hide a basic business problem

If a listing is wrong, fix the listing. If a vendor is underperforming, address the vendor. If ownership is unclear, fix ownership. If the data is unreliable, reconcile it before automating anything on top of it.

Deterministic automation handles calculations, data movement and alerts. AI handles classification, interpretation, drafting and unstructured information. AI is never asked to guess reorder mathematics or financial calculations.

Engagement Philosophy

Teaching by Building

We design every workflow, integration and safeguard with you in working sessions. Nothing gets handed over as a finished black box. By the end, the Eco Nappii team should be able to:

  • Read any business function and work out where automation belongs, and where it doesn’t
  • Design a workflow on a whiteboard before touching a tool
  • Brief a technical builder intelligently and judge whether what comes back can be trusted
  • Know what an AI agent can safely be trusted with, and what it can’t
  • Build in guardrails by default: human checkpoints and audit trails from the start
  • Apply the same method to a different founder’s business afterwards
Architecture, not software engineering

The capability being transferred is business-systems architecture: reading a function, spotting the bottleneck, designing the future-state workflow, briefing a technical builder intelligently, and judging whether the result is trustworthy. Ruppell ZA leads the technical implementation itself. You are not being trained to debug APIs or maintain automation scenarios, and you won’t be left holding them.

Guiding Principle

Automate the movement of the right information to the right person at the right time. Nothing gets automated just because it can be.

Every automation we propose has to answer one question: what commercial decision does this speed up or improve? If it has no answer, it doesn’t get built.

Phase 0 · 15–20 hrs

Validation & Architecture,
Not Rediscovery

You have already mapped your systems, bottlenecks and opportunities, and sent that through. Phase 0 builds on that work rather than repeating it. Our job is to test the diagnosis, check whether the numbers underneath it can be trusted, and rank what should actually be built.

  • Validate your existing mapping

    We work through the internal mapping you’ve already done, confirm what holds up, and say plainly where we think the diagnosis is incomplete or wrong.

  • Data-trust map for the critical numbers

    For each commercial number the business runs on: where it comes from, who owns it, how it is reconciled, and how much confidence it deserves. Nothing gets automated on top of a number we can’t vouch for.

  • One commercial number rebuilt from source

    We rebuild at least one number from the underlying data and compare it to what the business currently reports. This proves measurement integrity before anything is automated off it.

  • Commercially ranked opportunity list

    Every opportunity is sorted into one of four buckets: fix manually, automate, AI, or do not build. Ranked by commercial value, not by which function it sits in.

  • A recommendation for the first workflow

    One workflow, with the rationale for choosing it, a fixed scope and cost, the KPI it will be judged on, the controls around it, and a named internal owner at Eco Nappii.

The CRM Question

Do you actually need a CRM?

We don’t assume you do. Phase 0 gives you an honest answer, based on the data map rather than a preference for any product.

The options on the table:

  • A central CRM or hub that everything reports into
  • A lighter connective layer: a data warehouse plus an automation layer
  • One system of record with the others feeding into it

We come back to this in the Stack step, once the real data flows are clear. It isn’t a purchase anyone makes in week three.

What Phase 0 hands you
  • Business architecture map — all eight functions, their systems, how data flows between them and where it gets stuck, validated against your own mapping
  • Data-trust map — source, owner, reconciliation method and confidence level for every critical commercial number
  • One number rebuilt from underlying data, with the working shown
  • Ranked opportunity list — fix manually / automate / AI / do not build
  • First workflow recommendation — fixed scope, fixed cost, KPI, controls and internal owner
Phase 0 can end with “don’t build this yet”. If the data underneath an opportunity isn’t trustworthy, or the problem is a process or ownership problem rather than a systems one, we will say so and tell you what to fix first. That is a successful Phase 0, not a failed one.

Safeguards Framework

Every Automation Is
Classified Before It Ships

Before any automation or AI agent goes live, we agree how much it is allowed to do without a person involved.

Tier 1

Autonomous

Safe to run without anyone reviewing it.

For exampleA dashboard refresh, an internal alert
Tier 2

Human-approved

AI drafts it, and a person approves it before it goes out.

For exampleCustomer-facing replies, financial reports, ad budget changes
Tier 3

Human-only, AI-assisted

AI provides the analysis and options. A person makes and carries out the decision.

For examplePricing changes, supplier negotiations, hiring
Staged Rollout

Systems earn autonomy in stages. Nothing starts at the far end of this line.

Read

Systems collect and surface information. Nothing acts on it.

Recommend

Systems analyse and suggest. A person decides.

Prepare

Systems prepare an action and hold it for human approval.

Execute

Limited, in the early phase, to low-risk actions you have explicitly approved.

What stays under human control in the early phase

Material financial actions, purchase orders, pricing, employment decisions, sensitive customer communication and significant ad-budget changes. These are not automated in the first phase, whatever the tiering might otherwise allow.

And for every workflow

Whatever its tier, each workflow is documented with four answers before deployment.

Failure mode

What happens if the automation is wrong or the integration breaks?

Audit trail

Where is the log of what the system did, so it can be checked?

Kill switch

How can Eco Nappii, or the team, switch it off instantly if something looks wrong?

Data access boundaries

What each agent or tool can see, and what is explicitly off limits, such as financial credentials and customer personal information.

Stack Decision Principles

No Tools Chosen Upfront

Tooling is decided per workflow, against the same five criteria, once we know what the data actually needs to do.

  1. Does an existing tool already do this well enough?

    The first job is to avoid adding more tools than the business needs.

  2. Connection problem, or missing capability?

    If there’s a gap, is it because two systems don’t talk to each other (an integration layer fixes that), or because the capability doesn’t exist yet (a new tool fixes that)?

  3. Build vs. buy vs. connect

    An automation platform such as n8n, a native integration, or custom work against an API. Whichever genuinely fits the job.

  4. Total cost of ownership

    Who maintains this once we leave: Ruppell ZA on a retainer, someone at Eco Nappii, or does it need to be simple enough that nobody has to?

  5. Reversibility

    Can it be undone or swapped out without breaking the rest of the system?
